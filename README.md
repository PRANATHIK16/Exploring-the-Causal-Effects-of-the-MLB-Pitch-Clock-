
# Exploring the Causal Effects of Introducing the Pitch Clock to the MLB

The following repository contains codes for data, code for visuals, and tidy datasets for our Stat 184 project where we utilize our accummilated skills over the semester. The project explores the consequences of the MLB imposing a pitch clock in the 2023 season.

## Overview

While there are many ways we could examine the affects of a rule change, we specifially want to look at the following two stats of an MLB baseball game:

1. How long a game takes from start to finish.
2. How many spectators attend the game.

We Want to specfically see if the rule change of adding this pitch clock had resulted in games happening faster and in turn, did the faster pace games improve attendence among fans who would be more willing to watch these games. To do this, we will compare two seasons before the rule change, and two seasons after. 

### Interesting Insight

An interesting insight we had found quickly into our data anlysis was that after the pitch clock was introduced, the times of baseball games had fell significantly. We can see this change in our summary table:

![Table 1: Statistical Summary Table for Duration of Game](images/clipboard-1203091941.png)

The last 2 seasons saw the expected time length of a game drop by about 27 minutes and continued to drop into the 2024 season. We can also see that the rule change significantly cut down on games being significantly longer as no game after the rule change went over 4 hours and 24 minutes.

As discussed in our report, while we cannot absolutely assert that the rule change is the sole reason for this change, given that this was the most significant change going into the 2023 season, it is safe to say that the pitch clock helped make games go by faster.
